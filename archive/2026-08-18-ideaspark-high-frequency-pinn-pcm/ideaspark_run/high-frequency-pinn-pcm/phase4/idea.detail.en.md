# Kinetics-Clock PINNs for Endogenous High-Frequency VO2 Switching Fronts

**Method.** Kinetics-Clock Physics-Informed Neural Network (KC-PINN)

## Motivation
**Problem framing.** The structural problem is that an endogenous switching law can compress most phase change into a small, spatially moving portion of physical time. In a two-dimensional VO2 device, different locations therefore advance through the transition at different rates while voltage, temperature, and phase remain tightly coupled. A network expressed only in global physical time must represent a sharp front and smooth background with the same coordinate, exposing the high-frequency and residual-conditioning weaknesses highlighted by arxiv:2602.19265v1 and the adaptive-spectral diagnosis in arxiv:2605.04502v1.

The closest architectural precedent, NeuSA (arxiv:2509.04966v2), adds a global spectral state and causal evolution for canonical wave problems, but it does not construct a spatially local clock from the material kinetics. The energy-dissipation phase-field PINN in openalex:W4403555504 avoids propagation failure by solving a static steady-state problem, so it cannot represent pulse-created event time or moving topology. KC-PINN instead uses a finite analytic Gaussian basis with error-function primitives to construct the local clock without numerical time quadrature: the original electro-thermal-phase equations remain authoritative, and every first- and second-order derivative is pulled back through the learned map, including all spatial Hessian and cross terms created by a clock that varies across the device.

**Why now.** Three recent strands make this narrow gap actionable at once: arxiv:2605.04502v1 isolates gradient-scaling effects in adaptive spectral PINNs, arxiv:2602.19265v1 consolidates the high-frequency failure mechanism, and semanticscholar:a1448fd2600739b73894f3b8d467bf9a9e9630b2 exposes sub-nanosecond VO2 electro-thermo-phase topology as the relevant physical regime. A named FEniCSx model can now supply controlled synthetic trajectories, while automatic differentiation can evaluate the complete local-coordinate pullback rather than approximating or dropping its cross terms. The opportunity is therefore not merely to add spectral capacity, but to test whether an endogenous kinetics-aligned coordinate removes the specific representation bottleneck under a retrieval-bounded novelty claim.

**Why prior work stopped.**
- `arxiv:2509.04966v2` (arXiv 2025): NeuSA (arxiv:2509.04966v2) represents canonical initial-value problems with a truncated global spectral state evolved by a neural ordinary differential equation and causal physics constraints.
  - _Did not do_: It did not learn a spatially local monotone clock tied to the material phase-rate law or pull a coupled electro-thermal-phase device PDE through that clock.
  - _Structural reason_: Its representation assumes a global spectral evolution coordinate and therefore lacks the endogenous, spatially heterogeneous kinetics signal needed to define local material time.
- `arxiv:2605.04502v1` (arXiv 2026-05): The adaptive-spectral study (arxiv:2605.04502v1) varies Fourier frequencies and initial-condition gates to diagnose gradient scaling in a stiff nonlinear ordinary differential equation.
  - _Did not do_: It did not transform the independent time coordinate of a two-dimensional coupled device PDE or retain the chain-rule terms induced by a spatially varying map.
  - _Structural reason_: Its diagnostic setting has no spatially resolved phase-kinetic right-hand side from which an endogenous local clock could be constructed and tested.
- `openalex:W4403555504` (arXiv (Cornell University) 2024): The energy-embedded phase-field PINN (openalex:W4403555504) predicts steady ferroelectric microstructures by enforcing energy dissipation in a static formulation.
  - _Did not do_: It did not retain pulse-driven dynamic evolution, switching time, or moving phase-front topology in the learned solution.
  - _Structural reason_: It handles dynamic PINN propagation failure by replacing the trajectory problem with a steady variational target rather than by changing how physical time is represented.
- `semanticscholar:a1448fd2600739b73894f3b8d467bf9a9e9630b2` (arXiv 2026-04): The operando VO2 study (semanticscholar:a1448fd2600739b73894f3b8d467bf9a9e9630b2) resolves electrically steered conduction topologies and sub-nanosecond electro-thermo-phase dynamics.
  - _Did not do_: It did not provide a PINN coordinate mechanism that solves the governing device fields or isolates kinetics-specific representation value on synthetic numerical evidence.
  - _Structural reason_: Its contribution is physical measurement and mechanism discrimination, not a differentiable PDE solver or a training coordinate derived from the measured kinetics.

**What changes when the gap closes.** Closing the gap would make pulse-created event time, moving front topology, and electro-thermal energy closure jointly representable by one two-dimensional PINN without replacing the underlying equations with a static surrogate. That capability would connect the dynamic target regime exposed by semanticscholar:a1448fd2600739b73894f3b8d467bf9a9e9630b2 to a solver that can be evaluated on whole held-out devices or protocols, while directly testing whether the global spectral remedy of arxiv:2509.04966v2 is insufficient when kinetics are spatially heterogeneous. It would also establish a reusable conditional statement: when the local phase-rate magnitude is informative and the pullback is complete, coordinate adaptation can be attributed to material kinetics rather than to generic extra capacity.

## Method
**Pipeline.** KC-PINN first freezes a nondimensional two-dimensional VO2 electro-thermal-phase contract, including pulse interfaces, so the coordinate cannot silently alter the physics. A coefficient network $`a_{psi}(x)`$ instantiates an analytic Gaussian-basis cumulative clock whose error-function primitives make `tau_psi(x,0)=0` and $`partial_{t}$ tau_psi>0` exact, with `kfloor=sqrt(eps_dtype)*kref` and $`M_{tau}`$ selected only from `{8,16,32}` on training-validation cases. A capacity-matched field head predicts voltage, temperature, and phase from `(x,tau_psi,p(t))`; automatic differentiation constructs the complete first- and second-order temporal pullback and full spatial Hessian before the unchanged physical equations and $`r_{tau}=partial_{t} tau_{psi}-sqrt(K_{xi}^{2}+kfloor^{2})`$ are evaluated. All choices are locked before whole-case OOD scoring, and the matched identity intervention fixes `tau=t` while disabling $`r_{tau}`$ to test whether any downstream gain requires the kinetics clock.

### M1_analytic_clock
*Construct the exactly monotone local clock from a spatial coefficient network and analytic Gaussian error-function primitives.*

2. **Construct the analytic positive-basis clock** (`S2`)
   - Define a `ClockBasisSpec` containing the nondimensional time interval, fixed Gaussian centers $`c_{j}`$, common spacing `w`, floating-point type, `eps_dtype`, strictly positive `kref`, derived `kfloor`, and candidate set `{8,16,32}`. 【author decision: lock $`c_{j}`$, `w`, the floating-point type, and a strictly positive `kref` with units, source, and value before deriving $`kfloor=sqrt(eps_{dtype})\times kref`$.】 Evaluate a spatial-only coefficient network $`a_{psi}(x)`$ that returns exactly $`M_{tau}`$ coefficients and never reads a pulse value or predicted field. 【author decision: declare the coefficient-network input normalization, layers, activations, and the scalar training-validation score used to select $`M_{tau}`$; keep $`M_{tau}=16`$ as the default and resolve exact ties toward the smaller candidate.】 Apply softplus to each coefficient, evaluate every Gaussian basis $`b_{j}(t)`$ and its analytic error-function primitive $`B_{j}(t)`$, and form `tau_psi` exactly as E1. Obtain $`partial_{t}$ tau_psi`, spatial gradients, and the spatial Hessian by analytic basis derivatives plus automatic differentiation through $`a_{psi}(x)`$; do not use a finite time integral. Return a `ClockState` with coefficients, basis values, `tau_psi`, its derivative tensors, the selected $`M_{tau}`$, and the frozen `ClockBasisSpec`.

*The clock is evaluated by analytic Gaussian primitives, never finite time quadrature. Fixed knots $`c_{j}`$, spacing `w`, a declared positive reference rate `kref>0`, `kfloor=sqrt(eps_dtype)*kref`, and a training-validation-only choice $`M_{tau}$ in {8,16,32}` make `tau_psi(x,0)=0` and $`partial_{t}$ tau_psi=kfloor+sum_j $softplus(a_{psi},j)b_{j}(t)>0`$ exactly for the implemented map.*
$$ \tau_\psi(\mathbf{x},t)=k_{\mathrm{floor}}t+\sum_{j=1}^{M_\tau}\operatorname{softplus}(a_{\psi,j}(\mathbf{x}))B_j(t),\quad b_j(t)=e^{-((t-c_j)/w)^2},\quad B_j(t)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}w}{2}\left[\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{t-c_j}{w}\right)+\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{c_j}{w}\right)\right],\quad k_{\mathrm{ref}}>0 \tag{1} $$

   - _Why:_ The analytic construction gives `tau_psi(x,0)=0` and $`partial_{t}$ tau_psi=kfloor+sum_j $softplus(a_{psi},j)b_{j}(t)>0`$ exactly without finite quadrature, while the acyclic coefficient network never reads a predicted field.

### M2_fields_pullback_residuals
*Predict the coupled fields, perform the complete pulse-aware derivative transformation, and evaluate the unchanged physics plus direct kinetics-clock residual.*

3. **Predict the coupled fields in clock coordinates** (`S3`)
   - Represent the pulse protocol as a `PulseTrace` whose smooth segments expose `p(t)`, $`p_{dot}(t)`$, and $`p_{ddot}(t)`$, while every jump exposes separate left and right values and a stable $`interface_{id}`$. Pass `(x,tau_psi,p(t))` to one shared solution head $`h_{theta}`$ with three outputs. 【author decision: declare the capacity-matched solution-head trunk, layers, activations, input normalization, and output parameterizations for voltage, temperature, and phase.】 Store the outputs as $`V_{hat}`, `T_{hat}`$, and $`xi_{hat}`$ in a `FieldState`. Evaluate the physical spatial gradient of $`V_{hat}`$ through the composite map, set $`E_{hat}`$ from that gradient, apply the frozen conductivity to form $`J_{hat}`$, and contract current density with electric field to form $`QJ_{hat}`$; no learned auxiliary head may replace these deterministic relations. Return `FieldState` with the three fields, $`E_{hat}`, `J_{hat}`, `QJ_{hat}`$, the one-sided pulse record, and references to the clock and physical contract.
   - _Why:_ A single shared clock retains the electric-to-Joule-to-thermal-to-phase causal chain, while the known one-sided pulse value enters only the solution head and the clock graph remains acyclic.
4. **Pull back every required derivative** (`S4`)
   - Implement a `DerivativeBundle` for each scalar output of `h_theta(x,tau_psi,p)`. On an open pulse-smooth interval, use automatic differentiation to obtain partial derivatives with respect to direct spatial inputs, `tau_psi`, and every pulse component, together with all mixed and second derivatives required by E3 and E4. Assemble the physical first and second time derivatives with $`p_{dot}`, `p_{ddot}`$, the exact clock time derivatives, the mixed clock-pulse contractions, and the pulse Hessian contraction exactly as E3. Separately assemble the full physical $`2\times 2`$ spatial Hessian with both mixed spatial-clock terms, the clock-gradient outer product, and the clock Hessian exactly as E4. Preserve the full Hessian tensor; form a Laplacian only by taking its trace, and form each divergence from its complete vector or tensor operand. Never differentiate across a pulse jump: evaluate separate left and right traces using the `PulseTrace` interface record. Return physical-time derivatives, gradients, full Hessians, Laplacians, flux-ready derivatives, segment identifiers, and side labels.

*For each scalar `f=h_theta(x,tau_psi,p(t))`, the complete first- and second-order temporal pullback is evaluated on each open pulse-smooth interval. Pulse jumps are handled as interfaces and are never differentiated through.*
$$ \partial_t f=f_\tau\tau_t+f_p\!\cdot\dot p,\quad \partial_{tt}f=f_{\tau\tau}\tau_t^2+f_\tau\tau_{tt}+2(f_{\tau p}\!\cdot\dot p)\tau_t+f_{pp}[\dot p,\dot p]+f_p\!\cdot\ddot p \tag{2} $$


*The full physical-space Hessian, including both mixed contractions, the clock-gradient outer product, and the clock Hessian, is constructed before taking a trace or divergence; a Hessian is never conflated with its Laplacian.*
$$ D_{\mathbf{x}}^2f=f_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}}+f_{\mathbf{x}\tau}\otimes\nabla\tau_\psi+\nabla\tau_\psi\otimes f_{\tau\mathbf{x}}+f_{\tau\tau}\nabla\tau_\psi\otimes\nabla\tau_\psi+f_\tau D_{\mathbf{x}}^2\tau_\psi,\qquad \Delta f=\operatorname{tr}(D_{\mathbf{x}}^2f) \tag{3} $$

   - _Why:_ A space-dependent time coordinate preserves the declared PDE only when every induced chain-rule term is retained with the correct tensor shape; pulse jumps are handled as interfaces rather than differentiated through.
5. **Evaluate the original physics and kinetics-clock residuals** (`S5`)
   - At every interior point on an open pulse-smooth interval, evaluate the three entries of $`R_{phys}`$ exactly as E5 with the S4 derivatives and frozen S1 functions. Evaluate $`K_{xi}`$ with all declared $dependencies—`T_{hat}`, `E_{hat}`, `xi_{hat}`$, the full physical gradient of $`xi_{hat}`$, and its physical Laplacian—and form the sole auxiliary residual $`r_{tau}`$ directly from the exact clock derivative and the smooth positive kinetic magnitude in E2; introduce no log transform or centering operator. Form the physics-informed neural network (PINN) objective in E5 with the frozen $`s_{q}`$ and $`s_{tau}`$, nonempty initial, boundary, constitutive, and pulse-interface terms, and the clock indicator. At each pulse interface, pair records with the same physical point and edge time, impose continuity residuals for $`T_{hat}`$ and $`xi_{hat}`$, and evaluate the electric boundary-value residual independently on the left and right so $`V_{hat}`$ may follow the imposed step. Derive the energy-balance diagnostic separately by integrating the heat-storage, conductive-flux, and Joule terms with the physical-domain and boundary measures; report it without adding it to the training objective. Return a `ResidualBatch` with raw and normalized residual families, weights, case and interface identifiers, and the separate energy record.

*The sole auxiliary clock residual directly couples the exact clock derivative to the smooth positive phase-rate magnitude. The displayed normalized phase slope is conditional on vanishing phase and clock residuals; it is not an approximation bound.*
$$ r_\tau=\partial_t\tau_\psi-\sqrt{K_\xi(\hat T,\hat E,\hat\xi,\nabla\hat\xi,\Delta\hat\xi)^2+k_{\mathrm{floor}}^2},\qquad |\partial\hat\xi/\partial\tau|=|K_\xi|/\sqrt{K_\xi^2+k_{\mathrm{floor}}^2}<1\quad\text{when }r_\tau=0\text{ and }\mathcal{R}_\xi=0 \tag{4} $$


*The original electric, Joule-heat, and phase equations are evaluated using the complete pulled-back derivatives, with frozen residual scales and nonempty initial, boundary, constitutive, and pulse-interface terms. The clock term is disabled when the identity control sets `tau=t`.*
$$ \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\left(\nabla\!\cdot[\sigma(\hat T,\hat\xi)\nabla\hat V],\;\rho c_p\partial_t\hat T-\nabla\!\cdot[k(\hat T,\hat\xi)\nabla\hat T]-\sigma|\nabla\hat V|^2,\;\partial_t\hat\xi-K_\xi(\hat T,\hat E,\hat\xi,\nabla\hat\xi,\Delta\hat\xi)\right),\quad \mathcal{L}=\sum_q\|\mathcal{R}_q/s_q\|_2^2+\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{clock}}\|r_\tau/s_\tau\|_2^2+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IC/BC/interface}} \tag{5} $$

   - _Why:_ This preserves the declared electro-thermal-phase model while making the clock kinetics-specific; when both the phase and clock residuals vanish, $|\partial \xi /\partial \tau |=|K\xi |/sqrt(K\xi ^{2}+kfloor^{2})<1$ is a conditional property, not an approximation guarantee.
